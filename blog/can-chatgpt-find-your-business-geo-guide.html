Can ChatGPT Find Your Business? The UK Small Business Guide to GEO

Your customers now ask AI for recommendations instead of Googling. Here's how UK small businesses get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini — and how to check where you stand.

Something quietly changed in how customers find businesses like yours. Instead of typing "plumber near me" into Google and scrolling through ads, a growing share of people now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini a straight question — "who's a good plumber in Totnes?" — and take the answer they're given. One answer. No page two. If your business isn't in it, you don't exist for that customer.

What is GEO, in plain English?

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization. Where SEO was about ranking on a results page, GEO is about being the answer an AI engine gives. These engines don't show ten blue links; they synthesise a recommendation from sources they trust, usually naming just two or three businesses. The economics are brutal and simple: being cited is everything, and near-misses get nothing.

Why AI engines skip most small businesses

When an AI engine researches "best cafe in Kingsbridge", it leans on sources it can parse and verify: structured data on your website, consistent name-address-phone details across the web, review platforms, local directories and pages that answer questions directly. Most SME websites were built for Google's old rules — image-heavy homepages, vague copy, no structured data. To an AI engine, that's a blank. It will confidently recommend the competitor whose details it can actually read.

The five fixes that matter most

  1. Structured data. Add LocalBusiness schema markup to your site — your name, services, area, opening hours in a format machines read natively.
  2. Consistency everywhere. Your business name, address and phone number should be identical on your site, Google Business Profile, and every directory that lists you. Conflicting details make engines drop you.
  3. Answer real questions. A page that plainly answers "how much does a bathroom refit cost in Devon?" gets cited. A page that says "quality solutions for discerning clients" does not.
  4. Reviews with substance. Engines weigh review platforms heavily. A steady flow of specific, genuine reviews beats a wall of five-star one-liners.
  5. Be where the engines already look. Run a scan, see which sources get cited for your category, and make sure you're present on them.

How to check where you stand today

The test is simpler than most owners expect: ask the engines what your customers ask. "Best [what you do] in [your town]." "[Your business name] reviews." If you appear — good, defend it. If competitors appear instead, you now know exactly who's eating your inbound enquiries and where the engines found them. The window is still open

Most UK small businesses haven't heard of GEO yet, which is precisely the opportunity. The businesses that get their structured data, consistency and citable content in order over the next year will become the default answers in their area — and default answers are sticky. The ones that wait will be trying to displace an incumbent the AI already trusts. Checking where you stand takes a minute; being findable is fixable.
