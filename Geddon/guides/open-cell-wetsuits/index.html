OPEN-CELL WETSUITS, EXPLAINED.

What does “open cell” actually mean—and what changes when you put the suit on? Start with the inside of the neoprene, then consider fit, thickness and care.

By GEDDON · Updated 30 October 2026

What is an open-cell wetsuit?

In freediving and spearfishing, “open cell” usually describes an unlined neoprene surface on the inside of a wetsuit. Instead of a fabric lining against your skin, the soft neoprene sits directly against it. The outside can still have a fabric covering, camouflage print and reinforced panels.

Open cell describes that inner surface; it does not tell you the suit’s thickness, the quality of its pattern or how well it will fit. Those decisions still matter. An open-cell suit that gaps at the neck or pulls across your shoulders is not automatically a better choice than a well-fitting lined suit.

How should an open-cell freediving wetsuit fit?

Look for even contact through the torso and at the neck, wrists and ankles. You should be able to breathe comfortably, raise your arms and move into your usual swimming position. A suit should not restrict a full breath. Persistent gaps, strong pulling at the crotch or painful pressure are reasons to reassess the size or cut.

Measure against the maker’s size chart rather than relying only on your usual clothing size. A dedicated women’s cut can provide a different balance through the bust, waist, hips and torso; read our women’s freediving wetsuit fit guide for the measurements to check.

Does open cell mean a warmer suit?

A close-fitting unlined interior can help limit water movement against the skin. That does not create a universal warmth rating. Thickness, seals, fit, time in the water, activity and individual cold tolerance all affect the experience.

Compare those factors together. Our 5.5mm versus 7.5mm wetsuit guide explains the trade-offs without assigning a single temperature range to every diver.

Putting on and caring for an open-cell suit

  • Follow the maker’s instructions and use a lubricant suitable for the suit and your skin when required.
  • Remove jewellery and work with the pads of your fingers. Avoid pulling at exposed neoprene with your nails.
  • Ease the suit into position gradually. Do not force a tight section or stretch it by a small edge.
  • Rinse after use, dry out of direct sunlight and avoid prolonged folding or compression. Check the specific care instructions before using cleaners.
